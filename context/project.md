# DECOLONIAL.IST — project summary (source of truth for proposals)

**One line.** DECOLONIAL.IST (https://decolonial.ist) is an open knowledge base
of Russian colonialism that documents the tactics, techniques, and procedures
(TTPs) of colonization, structured on the MITRE ATT&CK model from cybersecurity
threat intelligence.

**What it is.** A matrix of 6 tactics (Legitimization of Domination,
Preparation for Seizure, Seizure of Governance, Defense Impairment,
Persistence, Extraction of Benefits) decomposed into 100+ techniques and
sub-techniques (e.g., T0143 Cultivation of an Imperial Diaspora, T0121
Artificial Famine, T0125 Forcible Removal of Children), each grounded in the
documented historical experience of peoples subjected to Russian aggression,
with campaigns and actor/instrument pages. Trilingual: Ukrainian, English,
Russian. Scope spans all peoples subjected to Russian/Soviet colonization:
Eastern Europe, the Caucasus, Central Asia, and the indigenous and minority
peoples within the Russian Federation — Tatars, Bashkirs, Buryats, Kalmyks,
Chuvash, Sakha, Circassians, Chechens, and many others.

**Mission.** Decolonizing knowledge: returning an independent voice to peoples
and societies that suffered colonization, and making the empire's recurring
mechanisms legible — historically and as they operate today.

**Why the ATT&CK structure matters.** It turns dispersed historical scholarship
into a rigorous, referenceable, machine-readable taxonomy — the same move that
made ATT&CK the lingua franca of cyber threat intelligence. It supports
comparative analysis across colonized peoples, pattern-matching between
historical and current Russian operations, and reuse by researchers,
journalists, educators, and OSINT/disinformation analysts.

**Status & practicalities.**
- Public release: August 2026 (site live in development since earlier).
- License: CC BY 4.0; free for any person or organization. No cookies, no
  tracking. Engine: fork of the MITRE ATT&CK Website (Apache 2.0), open on
  GitHub.
- Team: volunteer collective; grew out of the educational course *Russia and
  Decolonization: An Introduction* (https://decolonisation-ru.com), running
